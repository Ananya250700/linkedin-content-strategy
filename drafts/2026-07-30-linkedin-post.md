Most corporate wellness content is written for the person who already believes in wellness.

HR champions, L&D leads, people who already got the budget approved.

Nobody is writing for the CFO sitting across the table from them.

That gap shows up clearly in the data. At Vantage Fit, the content pieces driving the highest engagement from director-and-above HR titles aren't the ones about program design or employee experience. They're the ones that frame wellness as a cost-avoidance argument — absenteeism, attrition, productivity loss.

HR leaders don't need to be sold on wellness. They need content that helps them sell it upward.

We published a deep-dive on how manufacturing companies are building that internal business case for wellness programs. Link in comments.

But the broader point: if your wellness content isn't helping HR champions make the case to finance, it's not doing its job — no matter how well it ranks.

What does your highest-performing wellness content actually say? Genuinely curious.
