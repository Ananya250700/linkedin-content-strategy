# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-10-09

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content that ranks ≠ wellness content that converts — most HR content teams are optimizing for the wrong metric

---

Most wellness content teams are optimizing for the wrong thing.

High traffic. Low conversion. That's the pattern I see across B2B wellness platforms — including the work I've done at Vantage Fit.

A blog about "10 ways to prevent burnout" might rank on page one. It might pull 5,000 monthly visits. But it's not moving HR leaders closer to a buying decision.

Here's why: informational wellness content and decision-stage wellness content are almost never the same piece. Most teams build one and expect it to do both.

The fix isn't better writing. It's a different brief.

Decision-stage content for HR leaders answers a different question. Not "what is burnout?" — but "how do I build a case for a wellness program my CFO will approve?" Not "why does wellbeing matter?" — but "what ROI metrics should I track in year one?"

The shift is from topic-first to job-to-be-done. What is this HR leader actually trying to accomplish when they land on this piece?

When I reframe a brief that way, the content changes. The CTA makes sense. The conversion lifts.

Are your wellness blogs written for search engines — or for the HR leader who's trying to make a real decision?
