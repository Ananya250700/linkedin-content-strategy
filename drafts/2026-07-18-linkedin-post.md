**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** Most wellness content briefs answer the wrong question — they define what to write but not who's deciding and what they need to act.

---

Most wellness content briefs answer the wrong question.

They tell writers what to write. They don't say who's reading, or what that person is actually trying to decide.

That gap produces content that's technically accurate and operationally useless.

Here's the brief structure I use before writing any HR-facing wellness piece:

**1. Name the reader's moment — not just their role.**
Not "HR manager." "An HR manager who just got asked by their CFO to justify the wellness budget" is specific enough to write to. The role is a category. The moment is a brief.

**2. State the decision the piece enables.**
Every useful post helps a reader do something: evaluate an option, build a case internally, rule out an approach. If a post doesn't enable a decision, it's information — not content strategy.

**3. Define what a good outcome looks like.**
Not a traffic target. What does this HR leader believe, know, or do differently after reading? If you can't answer that, neither can the writer.

Most wellness briefs skip all three. The result is polished, search-optimized content that generates traffic and loses pipeline.
