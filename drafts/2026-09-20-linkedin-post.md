Q4 budget reviews kill more wellness programs than burnout does.

Every October, HR teams defend their wellness spend in front of finance — and most walk in with the wrong data. Participation rates, satisfaction scores, event attendance. None of that answers the question finance is actually asking: *what did this cost us, and what did it return?*

We just published a guide on Vantage Fit that breaks down how to make that case — not with generic ROI claims, but with the specific financial levers wellness actually moves: absenteeism cost reduction, turnover-related savings, and productivity impact tied to measurable program outcomes.

The piece most wellness vendors skip: the ROI argument only holds if your program was designed to move those metrics from day one. Wellness programs built around participation goals don't retrospectively become business outcomes when budget season arrives.

If you're heading into Q4 with a wellness program that runs but doesn't report, this is worth reading before you build your renewal deck.
