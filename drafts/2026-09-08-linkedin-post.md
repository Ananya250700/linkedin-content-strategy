Most content optimization starts with the keyword.

That's the wrong place to start.

Before I touch any blog at Vantage Fit, I run a 3-question check that has nothing to do with search volume:

**1. Is this piece earning the right kind of traffic?**
Not "how much traffic" — what kind. An HR manager doing discovery research is not the same as a CHRO evaluating platforms. Same keyword, different intent stage. One converts. One doesn't.

**2. Does the existing content match what the page actually ranks for?**
I've seen blogs rank for one keyword while answering a completely different question. That's a mismatch between SEO and editorial — and it usually explains why a page has solid rankings but a brutal bounce rate.

**3. Is the topic still relevant to the buyer we're targeting now?**
B2B wellness content has a shelf-life problem. A post written in 2022 about "return to office wellness" might still drive traffic — from a completely different audience than we need.

Only after those three pass do I look at keyword gaps, internal linking, or the call to action.

Optimizing a blog that fails these checks is just polishing something that won't convert.
