# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-13

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness content sounds safe, not useful — and HR leaders can tell the difference

---

Most corporate wellness content is written to sound safe. Not to be useful.

Your blog might rank. It might pull decent traffic. But if your HR audience reads it and thinks "that's nice" — not "I need to act on this" — you've built decorative content, not decision-support content.

Here's what I've noticed writing for HR decision-makers at Vantage Fit:

The posts that drive real engagement aren't the ones with a clean 5-step wellness framework. They're the ones that name a problem HR leaders recognize but have never seen articulated — burnout misread as disengagement, wellness programs that exist on paper but live nowhere in culture, ROI metrics everyone references and nobody actually tracks.

HR leaders are sophisticated readers. They've been sold wellness solutions for decades. They can smell a vendor perspective from the headline.

If your content doesn't acknowledge what's broken, you won't earn the credibility to offer the fix.

The sequencing matters more than the insight: challenge first, framework second. That's the only order that builds trust with this audience.

What's a piece of wellness content you've read recently that actually made you think differently?
