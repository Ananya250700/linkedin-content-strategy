# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-07

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing — versus publishing into the void

---

Most HR teams pick wellness topics based on what feels important — not what their employees actually search for.

The result: a content library full of "the importance of employee wellness" articles that nobody reads and no HR leader saves.

Before I write any wellness piece, I run the topic through three filters:

**1. Search intent match**
Is someone actively looking for this? "Employee wellness programs" = informational fluff. "How to reduce burnout in manufacturing teams" = decision-stage content. Different intent, different conversion potential.

**2. Funnel stage fit**
Does this topic serve someone awareness-stage or close-to-buying? Most wellness content teams overindex on top-of-funnel and have nothing for the HR leader who's two weeks from signing a contract.

**3. Honest angle availability**
Can you say something non-obvious? If your take is "wellness matters," skip it. If you can name a specific failure mode, a hidden tradeoff, or a counterintuitive insight — write it. That's the post that gets saved.

Wellness content that clears all three tends to rank, get bookmarked, and occasionally drive a demo request.

Content that fails all three usually gets rewritten once in 18 months, then quietly retired.
