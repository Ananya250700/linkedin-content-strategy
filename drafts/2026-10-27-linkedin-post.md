# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-10-27

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** What I check before optimizing a B2B blog — and why keywords aren't the starting point

---

Most content audits start with keywords. Mine starts with a different question: *Is this post actually helping someone make a decision?*

Keywords tell you what people are searching for. They don't tell you why a post with 8K monthly visits still generates zero demo requests.

Before I touch a single meta description, I run a 3-part check:

**1. Decision gap** — Does this post answer what an HR leader needs to know *before* evaluating a wellness solution? Traffic without intent is noise.

**2. Credibility signal** — Is there a data point, a real implementation example, or a practitioner voice in this piece? Or is it another "5 benefits of employee wellness" article that anyone could have written?

**3. Next step clarity** — What is the reader supposed to do after reading? If the answer is "nothing," the post is doing brand awareness work disguised as demand gen.

At Vantage Fit, I killed a high-traffic blog last quarter — not because it ranked poorly, but because it was answering the wrong question. The rewrite halved the traffic and tripled time-on-page.

Traffic isn't the goal. Decision-making support is.

What does your content audit actually measure?
