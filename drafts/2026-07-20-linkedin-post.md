# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-20

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Why your wellness blog drives traffic but not demo requests — the specificity gap

---

Your wellness blog gets 10,000 visits a month. Your demo pipeline doesn't reflect it.

That's not an SEO problem — it's a content problem.

Most corporate wellness content is optimized for search volume, not buyer intent. It ranks for broad terms like "employee burnout tips" or "mental health at work" and pulls in HR professionals who are researching, not deciding.

The content that actually converts looks different:

It names a specific pain. Not "burnout" — but "managers avoiding difficult conversations because HR feels like a threat."

It shows what bad looks like before it shows the fix.

It talks to one person — the CHRO who just lost a high performer and doesn't know why — not the entire internet.

Polished wellness blogs outrank genuinely useful ones all the time. Polished ones have keyword density. Useful ones have specificity.

Specificity converts. Broad doesn't.

Before you optimize your next wellness post for traffic, ask: who exactly is this for, and what do they need to believe to take the next step?

That question changes the brief entirely.
