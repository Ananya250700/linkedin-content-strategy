**Angle:** Manager burnout is systematically underreported in wellness surveys — making it the highest-leverage blind spot in most corporate wellness programs.

---

Manager burnout is the wellness data point most HR teams aren't measuring.

Wellness surveys are built for individual contributors. Managers take the same survey — and they almost always score higher on "I'm fine" metrics. Not because they are. Because they've spent years conditioning themselves to model resilience, not report it.

The result: your highest-leverage employee group goes unsupported until something breaks.

I wrote a piece for Vantage Fit on manager wellness — specifically what the data shows about how burnout behaves differently at this level. A few things that stood out:

- Managers are more likely to surface burnout in exit surveys than in active wellness check-ins
- Peer pressure and performance identity suppress reporting more than workload does
- Programs focused purely on individual behavior change miss the structural constraints managers actually operate under

Most wellness programs are designed from the bottom up. That's not inherently wrong — it just leaves a critical vulnerability at the layer that determines whether your culture actually lives the policy.
