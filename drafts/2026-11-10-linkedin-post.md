**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** What I actually look at before optimizing a blog — funnel fit over keyword rank

---

Most content audits start with traffic data.

Mine doesn't.

Before I touch a blog, I ask one question: Is this post trying to reach someone early in their research, or someone about to make a decision?

Traffic tells you if people showed up. It doesn't tell you if they left with what they needed.

At Vantage Fit, I work with HR-facing content — wellness programs, employee engagement, platform comparisons. The posts that rank well often target problem-aware searches: "signs of employee burnout," "workplace wellness statistics." Good for top-of-funnel. But HR buyers making a purchase decision need a different kind of content entirely.

Before I optimize anything, I look at:
1. Where in the funnel the post actually sits
2. Whether the keyword intent matches what we want readers to do next
3. Whether the CTA makes sense at that stage

A burnout statistics post doesn't need a "book a demo" CTA. A "best employee wellness platforms" post does.

Optimizing without funnel clarity just moves traffic around. It doesn't move buyers forward.

What's your process for deciding what's worth fixing vs. what should be retired?
