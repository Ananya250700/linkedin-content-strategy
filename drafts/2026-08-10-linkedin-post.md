# LinkedIn Draft — 2026-08-10

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness blogs that rank on Google don't convert HR buyers — because they're written to avoid the conversation HR leaders actually need to have.

---

Your wellness blog gets 5,000 visits a month and zero demo requests.

The problem isn't SEO. It's that most corporate wellness content is written for Google, not for the CHRO reading it at 11pm wondering why attrition jumped 18% last quarter.

Wellness content has a politeness problem. It avoids the real conversation:
— Burnout caused by management, not step count
— Attrition driven by toxic culture, not lack of yoga
— Emotional exhaustion from always-on expectations, not poor sleep hygiene

So instead, we publish "10 ways to boost employee engagement." Safe. Shareable. Forgettable.

HR leaders don't need validation. They need content that names what they're already living — and gives them a credible path forward.

The test I use before publishing any wellness piece:
1. Does it name something uncomfortable?
2. Does it give HR leaders a decision or an action, not just awareness?
3. Would a CHRO forward this to their CPO?

If the answer to any of these is no — the angle needs a rewrite, not the copy.

What's the last piece of wellness content you actually shared with your leadership team? I'm genuinely curious what made it worth forwarding.
