# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-18

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Most B2B wellness content is written for employees, not for the HR leaders who actually buy — the audience mismatch isn't in the keyword, it's in the job-to-be-done the content is solving for.

---

Most wellness content on B2B platforms isn't written for HR leaders. It's written for employees — and then sold as B2B content.

The difference matters more than most content teams admit.

An employee reading about managing workplace stress wants validation, quick tips, and a sense that someone understands what they're dealing with. An HR leader evaluating a wellness platform wants to know: what does implementation actually look like? What ROI evidence exists? How do similar organizations prove this worked internally?

These are different questions. They need different content.

At Vantage Fit, what I've seen actually perform with HR decision-makers: content that acknowledges the difficulty of building a business case, names the objections they'll face internally, and gives them language to answer those objections in a leadership meeting. Not "10 ways to reduce employee stress." Not a wellness glossary post.

What earns trust with a CHRO reading your blog:
— Case data from a comparable industry, not generic stat-stuffing
— An honest look at where wellness programs fail and what changed
— Content that treats them as the architect of the program, not a passive reader

If your wellness blog reads like it's talking to a burnt-out employee, your buyer will never recognize themselves in it.

The buyer and the beneficiary aren't the same person. Your content shouldn't treat them as if they are.
