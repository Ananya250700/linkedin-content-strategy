# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-24

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** High-traffic posts that don't convert are a liability — here's the 3-check framework I use before touching any blog optimization

---

A post ranking #2 for "employee wellness programs" drove 4,200 monthly visits. I still recommended we kill it.

Here's why.

Traffic without intent alignment is just vanity. That post was landing HR coordinators looking for program ideas — not decision-makers evaluating a platform. It had zero demo-path CTAs, competed with our own pillar page, and kept pulling in the wrong stage of the funnel.

Before I touch any optimization, I check three things:

**1. Who is actually landing here?**
Not the keyword — the person behind the search. Informational intent and commercial intent look identical in a traffic report.

**2. Does the content match the buyer stage?**
"What is employee wellness" and "best employee wellness software" are completely different conversations. Most posts try to serve both.

**3. Is this moving someone toward a decision?**
If the answer is no, the post is an anchor on the site — not an asset.

The wellness content space has a traffic addiction problem. The question worth asking isn't "how do we get more visits?" It's "which pages are actually helping someone buy?"

What do you track beyond traffic to measure if a blog is working?
