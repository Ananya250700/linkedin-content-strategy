# LinkedIn Draft — 2026-08-09

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** The BOFU funnel gap: most wellness content clusters are top-heavy with awareness content, leaving HR decision-makers without the ammunition they need to win internal budget conversations.

---

Most wellness content teams have an awareness problem.

Not a lack-of-content problem. Not an SEO problem.

They've built entire content clusters defining burnout, explaining EAP programs, listing wellness benefits — and then wonder why HR leaders aren't requesting demos.

The issue: HR decision-makers don't need education. They need ammunition for budget conversations.

Decision-stage wellness content looks fundamentally different from what most brands publish:

→ Not "what is burnout" — but "how to calculate the cost of burnout for your CFO"
→ Not "why wellness matters" — but "how to build a wellness program on a constrained budget"
→ Not case studies — but objection-handler content that addresses why programs fail

A simple audit: Open your last 10 wellness posts. Tag each as awareness, consideration, or decision. If the ratio isn't roughly 50/30/20, you're feeding the top of the funnel while the bottom leaks.

The HR leader who approves a wellness platform doesn't need to be convinced it matters. They already know. They need content that helps them win the internal argument.

Write for the meeting they're about to walk into — not the one they had two years ago.

What does your wellness content-to-decision ratio look like?
