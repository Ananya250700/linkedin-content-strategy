# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-28

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I flagged a high-traffic post for a complete rewrite — and what the decision actually came down to

---

A blog ranking #3 for a high-volume keyword is not automatically worth keeping.

Last quarter, I flagged a post at 2,000+ monthly visits for a full rewrite. The keyword was solid. The ranking held. But when I looked at who was landing on it — and what they did next — none of it mapped to our buyer.

The post was answering a question employees Google during a personal crisis. Not a question HR leaders have when they're evaluating a wellness platform.

Traffic that can't move pipeline is a liability. It inflates session counts, skews engagement data, and gives you false confidence about topical authority.

Three questions I now run before optimizing anything:

1. Is the search intent from someone who could recommend or buy the platform?
2. Does this content move them closer to a decision — or just satisfy curiosity?
3. If we doubled this traffic tomorrow, would any business metric shift?

That post didn't get deleted. I rewrote it for the HR leader researching burnout prevention programs — same URL, completely different angle, different CTA.

Two months later, the assisted conversions told a different story.

What's your filter for deciding when high-traffic content is actually worth your time?
