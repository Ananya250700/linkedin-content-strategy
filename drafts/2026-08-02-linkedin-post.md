**Angle:** Wellness content calendars are built around SEO volume, not the HR calendar — and that mismatch explains why content peaks in January but converts in July.

---

Wellness content performs best in January. Most of it is written for a problem HR leaders feel most acutely in July.

HR leaders search for burnout resources right before Q3 performance cycles — when the year's actual workload reality becomes visible. Not in January, when everyone's optimistic.

But most wellness content calendars are built around SEO volume, not the HR calendar. The result: excellent evergreen content that peaks in January and misses the moments that actually convert — Q3 planning, performance review season, headcount freeze announcements.

We tested this at Vantage Fit. Content mapped to a specific HR decision moment ("what do I do with a team showing early burnout signals before review season") consistently outperforms the evergreen version of the same topic. Not because it's better written. Because it arrives when the problem is already on someone's desk.

We published a mid-year wellness audit guide this week — built specifically for the Q3 planning window HR teams are in right now. Covers early warning signals, a 30-minute team pulse approach, and how to document findings in a way that gets leadership attention.

Link in comments.

If you run a wellness content program: when does your content calendar peak? If it's not mapped to when HR leaders actually need resources, you're optimizing for the wrong metric.
