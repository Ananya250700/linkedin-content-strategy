# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-10-29

**Pillar:** 4 — Vantage Fit Work Amplification
**Angle:** What building wellness content for frontline workers revealed about the gap in corporate wellness content — specificity converts, and most vendors ignore the segment entirely.

---

Most corporate wellness content is written for desk workers.

Step counters. Meditation apps. Mental health webinars. The standard package — designed for someone with a laptop and a calendar block.

But when I built Vantage Fit's content for manufacturing and frontline HR teams, the data pushed back.

These employees aren't disengaged because they lack wellness programs. They're disengaged because every wellness initiative they've encountered was built for someone else's workplace.

The implementation guide I worked on for manufacturing HR — shift-worker schedules, physical fatigue, no desktop access — consistently outperforms our general wellness content on time-on-page and scroll depth.

The reason: specificity converts.

HR leaders managing a factory floor aren't searching for "employee wellness tips." They're looking for someone who understands rotating shifts, physical recovery, and digital access constraints. When the content speaks to their actual problem, it stops being content and starts being a resource.

That's the gap most B2B wellness vendors aren't filling — not because they can't, but because writing for a niche audience feels risky.

It isn't. Broad content gets traffic. Specific content gets saved, shared, and acted on.

Which workforce segment does your wellness content currently ignore?
