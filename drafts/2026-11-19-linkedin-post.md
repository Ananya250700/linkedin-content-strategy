**Angle:** Participation rates ≠ health outcomes — and most wellness programs are measuring the easier metric

---

Here's something most wellness vendors don't publish:

Participation rates and health outcomes are not the same metric. Most corporate wellness programs track participation because it's easier to report — not because it's what HR leaders actually need to justify budget.

At Vantage Fit, this gap shapes the content I build. Because when HR leaders enter a vendor evaluation, the question isn't "how many employees joined the challenge?" It's: "Will this reduce absenteeism? Can I show this to the CFO?"

The same logic applies to wellness content strategy. Posts that lead with outcomes ("teams using daily wellness check-ins reported 23% lower absenteeism in Q3") perform differently than posts that lead with activity metrics. Because the audience — CHROs, People leaders — knows the difference. They've sat through enough vendor demos.

If you're evaluating a wellness program or building content around one, ask this first: What's the outcome metric? Not the engagement metric.

That one question changes what you write, what you track, and what gets approved in your next budget cycle.

What does your leadership team ask about first when evaluating wellness ROI — participation or outcomes?
