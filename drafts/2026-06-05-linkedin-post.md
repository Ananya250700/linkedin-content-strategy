# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-05

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content that ranks but doesn't convert isn't a distribution problem — it's a trust problem caused by overly safe, friction-free messaging.

---

Your wellness content is ranking. HR leaders aren't converting.

That's not a distribution problem. It's a trust problem.

Most corporate wellness content is written to feel safe — uplifting tone, no mention of leadership failure, no acknowledgment of why wellness programs actually fail. It's designed to avoid friction, not create clarity.

But HR leaders making a real purchase decision aren't looking for inspiration. They're looking for evidence that you understand their situation — the burnout skeptics on their leadership team, the budget they have to justify, the employees who've stopped believing in wellness initiatives.

Content that acknowledges the hard parts does more than educate. It signals: *we know what you're actually dealing with.*

The most-converted pieces I've worked on at Vantage Fit weren't the cleanest. They named the problem honestly, showed the gap between what companies promise and what employees experience, and gave HR leaders a framework they could use in a real conversation with their CHRO.

Traffic is a vanity metric if your content never makes a reader feel seen.

What kind of wellness content has actually helped you make a decision — or share with your team?
