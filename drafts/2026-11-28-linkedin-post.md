# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-28

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing about — most teams pick based on search volume or gut feel, not on whether HR leaders can actually act on the information.

---

Most wellness content calendars are built on the wrong question.

Teams ask: "What are people searching for?"

The right question is: "What does an HR leader need to believe or decide before they can act?"

Those are not the same thing.

Here's the 3-part filter I use before committing to any wellness topic:

**1. Is there a decision to make?**
If the content doesn't help HR leaders choose, prioritize, or justify something — it's informational noise. Educating isn't enough.

**2. Does the problem have a named owner?**
"Employee burnout" is a vague concern. "A CHRO trying to justify a wellness budget to a skeptical CFO" is a problem with an owner. Write for the second one.

**3. Is the content you're competing with actually good?**
Most wellness content in this space is thin. If the top results are generic listicles, there's a real opportunity — but only if you go deeper on the angle, not just the word count.

A topic passes the filter only when all three are yes.

Most wellness blogs skip step 1 entirely. That's why they rank but don't convert.

What's your filter for picking wellness topics worth writing?
