The wellness blog that got the most LinkedIn shares from our content last quarter wasn't about employee benefits.

It was about how managers talk about burnout wrong.

We published a piece on Vantage Fit documenting the specific phrases that feel supportive but actually minimize what employees are experiencing — things like "everyone's going through it" or "just take a mental health day."

HR leaders shared it more than any benefits rollout guide we ran. Because it named something real. Not a policy gap. A language problem they recognized in their own one-on-ones.

That's the pattern I keep seeing in B2B wellness content: the pieces that convert are the ones that say the uncomfortable thing plainly. HR leaders already know what's broken. The content that earns their trust is the content that names it without softening it into a tip.

If your wellness blog is getting traffic but no demo requests, check whether the content is honest — or just safe.

What's the last piece of wellness content you shared because it said something you weren't expecting?
