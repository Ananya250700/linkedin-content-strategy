Most wellness content is written for desk workers.

Which would be fine — except roughly 80% of the global workforce doesn't sit at a desk.

Frontline workers in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and retail have almost no wellness content written specifically for them. What exists is generic, ergonomics-focused, or borrowed from office-first frameworks that don't translate to the shop floor.

We published a manufacturing wellness guide at Vantage Fit that required a different approach entirely: understanding shift structures, the role of floor supervisors in program adoption, and why standard pulse surveys fail when 60% of your workforce doesn't have a company email.

Three things HR teams in manufacturing consistently get wrong on wellness:

1. Assuming digital-first delivery works when device access is inconsistent
2. Measuring program success through app engagement when real adoption depends on manager buy-in
3. Treating physical safety culture as separate from wellness — it's the same conversation

Frontline wellness isn't a niche. It's an underserved majority.

If you're building a wellness strategy for an ops-heavy or manufacturing workforce, what's been the hardest thing to get right?
