# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-04

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** High-ranking wellness content often generates zero pipeline — because traffic intent and buyer intent are not the same thing.

---

A blog ranked #2 for a high-volume keyword. 8,000 monthly visitors. Zero demo requests in six months.

The content team called it a success. The sales team had never heard of it.

This is one of the most common failure modes in B2B wellness content — and it's almost always a positioning problem, not a writing problem.

Wellness content that ranks well tends to be broad, educational, and optimized for search intent that doesn't match the buyer's stage. "What is employee wellness" brings traffic from HR assistants doing research. It doesn't reach the CHRO deciding which platform to shortlist.

The fix isn't to write less — it's to audit who you're actually writing for at every stage of the funnel.

Three questions before any wellness blog brief:
1. Who is the primary reader? (Role, not persona)
2. What decision does this content support?
3. Is this search intent — or buyer intent?

Traffic is not traction. The metric that matters is whether your content moves someone closer to a conversation.

What does your wellness content actually measure for success?
