# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-12

**Pillar:** 4 — Vantage Fit Work Amplification
**Angle:** Most wellness programs are engineered for desk workers — frontline and manufacturing employees are an afterthought, and the data gap proves it.

---

Most corporate wellness programs are designed for employees who have a desk.

The apps, the pulse surveys, the lunchtime mindfulness sessions — they assume fixed schedules, reliable internet, and the option to step away when stress peaks. That's a minority of the global workforce.

For manufacturing and frontline workers, the landscape is different: shift fatigue, physical safety culture, variable pay stress, and a near-complete absence of peer recognition infrastructure. The standard wellness toolkit doesn't transfer. It gets diluted.

We published a piece on Vantage Fit on how manufacturers are building programs that actually work for shop floor employees — not adapted desk-worker models, but built from scratch for this context.

One finding that deserves more attention: companies that rate themselves highly on "wellness investment" are often the same ones with the widest access gap between desk and non-desk employees. They're investing in wellness — just not for the workforce that needs it most.

The gap isn't a budget problem. It's a design problem. Wellness strategy was built around one type of worker and never rebuilt for everyone else.
