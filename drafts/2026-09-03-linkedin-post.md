Most companies report high participation in their wellness programs. Most also report low impact on retention and absenteeism.

Those two facts aren't a contradiction. They're what happens when you measure the wrong thing.

Participation rate tells you how many people showed up to the webinar. It doesn't tell you whether the program changed a single health behavior, reduced stress claims, or kept a key hire from burning out and leaving.

At Vantage Fit, we spent months mapping how HR teams actually evaluate wellness ROI — and the gap between what gets tracked and what matters is significant.

The metrics that signal real program health:
— Sustained behavior change over 60+ days (not one-week challenges)
— Health risk assessment completion rates and score trajectory
— Manager participation as a leading indicator (managers model; teams follow)
— Correlation between wellness engagement and retention data

If your program's only proof of success is "320 people signed up," you don't have a data story. You have an attendance sheet.
