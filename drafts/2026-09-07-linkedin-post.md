# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-07

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Employees don't engage with wellness content because it's written to reassure leadership, not to reflect employee reality — and they can tell.

---

Employees don't read your wellness content because it doesn't tell the truth.

Not about burnout. Not about why they're actually exhausted. Not about what's pushing them out.

Most corporate wellness content is written to reassure — to signal that the company cares, that programs exist, that leadership is paying attention. It's written for the board deck, not for the person three Slack messages deep at 9 PM wondering if it's worth staying.

That's the real problem. Employees have a precise internal signal for content written to look good vs. content written to actually help them. They know in about four seconds.

The wellness brands that actually cut through aren't the ones with better design or a faster publishing cadence. They're the ones willing to name the uncomfortable things: the manager who never acknowledges effort, the culture that rewards staying late, the mental health day policy nobody uses because the workload doesn't pause.

Real wellness content makes someone feel seen first. Resources and solutions come after that.

If your content is getting pageviews but no real engagement, start here — what uncomfortable truth is it going out of its way to avoid?
