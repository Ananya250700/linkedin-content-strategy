# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-01

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I retired a 12,000-visit/month post — and what I built instead (high traffic ≠ pipeline value)

---

A post with 12,000 monthly visitors was hurting our pipeline. So I retired it.

That sounds counterintuitive until you see what it was actually doing.

The post ranked for "office exercise ideas" — a top-of-funnel wellness query with high volume and zero commercial intent. People landed, skimmed, and left. No demo requests. No email signups. No scroll depth past the third tip.

In B2B wellness content, traffic that doesn't move toward a decision is just vanity metric management. HR leaders aren't buying a wellness platform because they read a listicle about desk stretches. They're buying because something convinced them the status quo isn't working.

So instead of optimizing the post, I rebuilt it.

New angle: "Why your office exercise program has 30% participation — and what that number actually tells you." Same keyword territory. Completely different buyer intent signal. The new version targets the question HR leaders are already asking internally, not the one they type into Google on a slow afternoon.

Three months later: lower traffic, higher average time on page, and two demo-intent clicks from organic that the old post never generated.

High traffic is a lagging indicator of the wrong strategy.

What do you actually optimize for when the numbers look good but the pipeline doesn't move?
