# LinkedIn Draft — 2026-07-03

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** High wellness blog traffic is a vanity metric if it's attracting the wrong audience — the content that ranks isn't the content that converts HR buyers.

---

A wellness blog ranking #1 on Google doesn't mean much if your buyer never reads it.

Most B2B wellness platforms chase traffic — page views, impressions, organic reach. And the content earns it. "10 ways to reduce workplace stress." "Benefits of employee wellness programs." Thousands of clicks. From whom? Employees looking for relief. Students doing research. Journalists. Not CHROs evaluating a software purchase.

The traffic is real. The intent isn't.

After nearly two years writing for HR decision-makers at Vantage Fit, here's what I've learned: the content that drives demo requests looks nothing like the content that drives traffic. High-converting content answers a different question — not "what is wellness?" but "how do I build a program that gets adoption, and how do I justify the ROI to leadership?"

That's a harder brief to write to. It requires knowing the buyer's objections, not just their interests.

Two questions worth auditing if you run wellness content:
→ Are your top 5 posts attracting readers or buyers?
→ What's the search intent behind your highest-traffic terms?

Traffic tells you what people are curious about. Intent tells you what they're ready to act on.

What does your bottom-of-funnel wellness content look like right now?
