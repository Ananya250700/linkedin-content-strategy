Most wellness ROI conversations ignore the one metric HR actually gets judged on: manager retention.

Employees don't quit companies. They quit managers who are themselves burned out.

I spent several weeks building out the manufacturing wellness content cluster at Vantage Fit — reviewing shift patterns, reading what HR teams in the sector report upward, and mapping where wellness programs actually break down.

The pattern was consistent: organizations measuring ROI through absenteeism rates or productivity scores were missing the manager layer entirely. By the time those numbers surfaced in a quarterly report, the real damage — team attrition, knowledge loss, morale collapse — had already happened six months earlier.

The organizations getting it right had one thing in common: they tracked manager wellbeing as a leading indicator, not a footnote in an annual survey.
