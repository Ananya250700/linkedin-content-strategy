**Angle:** The vanity metric trap in corporate wellness — most programs measure participation, not behavior change, and the distinction explains why so many wellness investments fail to show ROI.

---

Most wellness programs are measuring the wrong thing.

Participation rate is the vanity metric of corporate wellness. It tells you who signed up. It doesn't tell you who changed.

At Vantage Fit, the pattern we see consistently: HR leaders running genuinely effective programs don't lead with enrollment data. They track behavior over time — activity consistency, sleep quality trends, stress self-reports — at 30, 60, 90 days post-launch.

The gap between a program with 80% participation and one that actually reduces healthcare costs usually comes down to a decision made at program design: did you build for signups or for habits?

Programs optimized for signups get strong Q1 numbers. Programs optimized for habits show up in Q3 health claims data.

If your wellness platform can't show 90-day behavior retention by cohort, you're reporting on optics — not outcomes.

What metric does your organization actually use to evaluate wellness ROI?
