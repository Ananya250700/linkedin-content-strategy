HR leaders track wellness participation. Almost nobody tracks what changes because of it.

We published a piece on this at Vantage Fit recently — and while writing it, I kept running into the same gap: companies celebrate 80% sign-up rates for wellness programs without ever asking whether any of those employees reported less burnout six months later.

Participation ≠ impact. But most wellness dashboards only show you the former.

What actually matters:
→ Did absenteeism go down?
→ Did voluntary turnover in high-stress roles change?
→ Do managers report fewer crisis escalations?

These aren't hard to measure. They're just inconvenient — because they take longer and reveal whether a program actually worked.

Most wellness vendors don't push clients to measure this way. It's easier to report "X employees completed the challenge" than to track downstream behavioral change.

The blog goes deeper on what a proper wellness measurement framework looks like. Link in comments.

If you're an HR leader evaluating a wellness platform — what metrics does your vendor actually report back to you?
