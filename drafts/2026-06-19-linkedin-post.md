# LinkedIn Draft — 2026-06-19

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness vendors keep answering "why does wellness matter" — CHROs accepted that three years ago. The question they're actually asking is harder, and almost nobody is answering it.

---

Most wellness vendor content answers a question nobody is asking.

"Why does employee wellness matter?" has been answered. CHROs know the business case. They accepted it three years ago.

The question they're actually asking in 2026 is: which interventions work in a hybrid workforce at scale — and how do I know before I spend the budget?

That's a harder question to answer honestly. Because honest answers require admitting what doesn't work, citing studies that complicate the case, and showing failure data alongside success data.

Most vendors won't do that. The content team's job is to generate demand, not complicate it.

So CHROs get 40 variations of "employee wellness drives productivity" and almost nothing that helps them make an informed choice between two competing approaches.

The brands building trust with HR buyers right now are publishing the difficult content — comparison frameworks, implementation pitfalls, ROI methodology breakdowns. Not because it's comfortable, but because it's what buyers actually need.

Sophistication isn't what you say about your platform. It's whether you help the buyer think, not just feel.

What's the last piece of wellness content you read that actually changed how you thought about something?
