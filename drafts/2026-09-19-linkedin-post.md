**Angle:** 80% of the global workforce is deskless — but nearly all corporate wellness content was written for office workers. The manufacturing wellness implementation guide addresses what actually works in shift-based, frontline environments.

---

80% of the global workforce doesn't sit at a desk. Most corporate wellness content assumes they do.

We recently built an implementation guide for manufacturing companies deploying workplace wellness programs — and the research made one thing clear: the frameworks HR leaders use for office-based wellness don't transfer to shift-based, frontline environments.

The problems are structural.

Shift workers can't join a midday wellness webinar. Frontline teams often don't have company email. Program engagement becomes difficult to track when employees rotate across facilities and shifts. Mental health conversations hit differently in a culture that historically deprioritizes vulnerability.

What actually works in manufacturing wellness is less about program content and more about access architecture — when employees can engage, on which device, in which language, and with what degree of anonymity.

The guide breaks down implementation across five manufacturing contexts. Each one is shaped by the specific constraints of that environment, not a generic HR playbook retrofitted from a white-collar setting.

If you're an HR leader in manufacturing trying to get past surface-level wellness, it's worth a read.
