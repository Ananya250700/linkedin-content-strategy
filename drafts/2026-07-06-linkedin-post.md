# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-06

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness blog ranking on page 1 is proof of traffic, not proof it's working — most B2B wellness content is built for visibility, not decision-making, and never reaches the HR leader who's actually evaluating vendors.

---

A wellness blog ranking #1 on Google is not proof it's working.

I've audited content that pulls 10,000 visits a month and generates almost no demo requests. The problem isn't the SEO. It's what the content is saying.

Most B2B wellness content is built for visibility, not decision-making. It answers "what is employee burnout" for someone who already knows the answer. It ranks because the topic has volume. But the reader it attracts — a general searcher — has no intent to buy a wellness platform.

HR leaders evaluating vendors don't Google "what is employee burnout." They Google "how to measure ROI on wellness programs" or "what a wellness platform actually tracks." That's BOFU intent. Almost nobody in this space is creating that content.

Here's the filter I use before writing anything:
- Who specifically is searching this?
- What decision are they trying to make?
- Does this content help them make it?

If you can't answer all three, the post will drive traffic and lose the sale.

The most dangerous wellness content is the kind that looks like it's working.

What does your top-traffic post actually convert?
