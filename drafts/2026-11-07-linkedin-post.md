# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-07

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-question filter for deciding whether a wellness topic is worth writing about — framed around what converts vs. what just gets clicks

---

Most wellness content teams pick topics by gut feel.

What sounds warm? What's trending? What did the CEO mention in a town hall?

The result: a blog archive full of "how to manage stress at work" posts that rank, get clicks, and convert nobody.

Before I write any wellness topic for Vantage Fit, I run it through 3 questions:

**1. Does this help an HR leader justify a decision to their CFO?**
If the post doesn't contain a stat, a case study, or a cost calculation — it's entertainment, not content.

**2. Is there a non-obvious angle?**
If a quick Google search returns 10 posts with the same title and structure, the topic needs a sharper edge before it earns a brief.

**3. Could any wellness vendor publish this exact post?**
If yes, it's not yours. Generic wellness content builds category awareness for your competitors, not authority for your brand.

Most topics fail at least one of these.

The ones that pass all three tend to drive demo requests — not because they're the most searched, but because they're the most useful to the person who signs the contract.

That's the distinction most wellness content teams miss: writing for the reader vs. writing for the decision-maker.

What filter does your team use before greenlighting a topic?
