# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-08-04

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I killed a high-traffic post — and what I replaced it with

---

A post pulling 4,000 monthly visits with zero demo influence is a cost center, not an asset.

Last quarter, I retired one of Vantage Fit's highest-traffic blogs. It ranked on page one for a broad wellness keyword. Solid click-through rate. Decent time on page.

But when I mapped it against actual conversion data — it was pulling in HR coordinators looking for inspiration, not CHROs evaluating a wellness platform.

Wrong audience. Wrong intent. Wrong funnel stage.

Here's what I looked at before making the call:

**1. Who's actually landing on the page**
Not demographics — behavioral signals. Content paths, exit patterns, what they clicked next.

**2. What they do after reading**
Do they move toward a product page or a case study? Or do they bounce?

**3. Can it be repositioned?**
Sometimes a TOFU post just needs a BOFU section added. This one couldn't be saved that way.

I redirected the URL to a more targeted implementation piece for People leads and rebuilt the CTA from scratch.

Traffic dropped 40%. Pipeline attribution improved.

If you're measuring wellness content performance by pageviews alone, you're optimizing for the wrong outcome.

What's the metric that actually moves the needle in your content program?
