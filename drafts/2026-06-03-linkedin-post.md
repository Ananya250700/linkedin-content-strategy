# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-03

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing about

---

Most HR content teams pick wellness topics by gut feel or Google Trends.

The result: blogs that rank, get read, and do absolutely nothing for the pipeline.

Before I write about any wellness topic at Vantage Fit, I run it through a 3-part filter:

**1. Does it map to a decision HR leaders actually face?**
Not "is burnout real" (everyone already knows). But "how do I justify a wellness budget to my CFO" — that's a decision. Content that answers real decisions earns bookmarks, not just clicks.

**2. Does it exist at the right funnel stage?**
Most wellness content stacks at the top — awareness, education, generic advice. If a piece doesn't move someone closer to action, it's not content strategy. It's publishing for publishing's sake.

**3. Can we own the specificity?**
Generic wellness articles lose to whoever ranks highest. But a post written for manufacturing employees or remote-first teams — that's a niche a vendor can actually own.

If a topic fails any of these three, we don't write it.

Which filter do you think most wellness content teams skip?
