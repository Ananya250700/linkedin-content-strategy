# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-22

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** The BOFU gap — most wellness content clusters cover awareness well but skip the decision-stage content that actually drives program commitment.

---

Most HR content teams can tell you which wellness post got the most traffic.

Almost none can tell you which piece helped a decision-maker actually commit to a program.

That gap is the entire problem.

Wellness content defaults to awareness: why mental health matters, what EAPs are, the ROI of wellbeing. Useful. Gets clicks. Almost never converts.

Bottom-of-funnel content — the pieces that help an HR leader say "yes, this is the right approach" — is almost always missing from the cluster.

Here's the three-question audit I use to check if a content cluster actually covers decision-stage intent:

**1. Does this piece answer "how does this work in a company like mine?"**
**2. Does it address the objection an HR leader would raise in a budget meeting?**
**3. Does it give a specific, implementable next step — not just a generic download?**

If your wellness content can't pass that filter, you're generating traffic from an audience that isn't ready to act — and leaving the audience that is ready with nothing to hold onto.

Traffic is vanity. Decision content is strategy.

What's the biggest BOFU gap you see in HR or wellness content?
