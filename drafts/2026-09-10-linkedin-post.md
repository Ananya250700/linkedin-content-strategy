Most corporate wellness programs run under 20% participation. Most vendors don't publish that stat. We did.

I was working on a piece for Vantage Fit this week on why wellness programs fail to drive engagement — and the pattern I kept finding wasn't about program design. It was about how these programs are communicated internally.

HR teams typically use vendor-provided content to promote wellness to employees. That content reads like vendor marketing. Employees notice.

When internal comms sound like a pitch, participation treats it as optional. Not because the program is bad — but because nothing in the messaging signals it was designed for them specifically.

What consistently shifts participation rates:
→ Specificity over aspiration ("this covers X" vs. "invest in your wellbeing")
→ Manager endorsement over HR broadcast
→ Proof of use over promise of value

The piece breaks down what the participation gap looks like across company sizes — and what high-performing programs do differently in the first 30 days of rollout.
