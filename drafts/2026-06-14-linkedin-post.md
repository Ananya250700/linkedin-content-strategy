# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-14

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** Most wellness content briefs start with a keyword, not a buyer decision — a 3-question framework that changes what gets written and why.

---

Most wellness content briefs I've seen start with a keyword.

That's the wrong starting point.

A keyword tells you what people search. It doesn't tell you what decision they're trying to make — or whether your content actually helps them make it.

Before I brief any wellness content piece, I answer three questions:

**1. Where is this reader in the decision process?**
Not "HR professionals." Specifically: are they discovering a problem, evaluating solutions, or trying to justify a purchase to leadership? The same topic requires completely different framing depending on the answer.

**2. What do they need to believe after reading this?**
Not what should they learn — what should they *believe*? A useful brief names one belief shift. "By the end of this post, an HR leader should believe their current wellness program is measuring the wrong thing."

**3. What action does this content support?**
Download. Demo request. Internal share. Nothing. If the answer is nothing, don't publish it.

Most wellness content teams skip this. Which is why most wellness content performs well on paper — traffic, rankings — and moves nothing in the funnel.

What does your content brief actually start with?
