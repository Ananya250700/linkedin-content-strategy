# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-16

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content is optimized for brand safety, not employee trust — and that's why it ranks but doesn't convert.

---

Most wellness content ranks. Almost none of it converts.

That's not a traffic problem. It's a trust problem.

When a corporate wellness blog avoids words like "burnout," "toxic manager," or "emotional exhaustion" — the very words employees type into search at 11pm — it signals something to the reader: this brand doesn't understand what I'm actually going through.

HR leaders often review wellness content for brand safety first. The result is a sanitized version of a real problem. It performs well in an editorial review. It performs poorly with the people it's meant to help.

The fix isn't authenticity theater. It's simpler: write about the problem your employees are actually searching for — not the version your brand feels comfortable claiming.

One filter I use: if a post could appear on any wellness vendor's blog without editing, it isn't specific enough to convert.

What uncomfortable truth does your wellness content still avoid?
