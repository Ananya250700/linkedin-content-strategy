# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-08-21

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content teams celebrate traffic. They shouldn't. The gap between ranking and converting is a strategy problem, not an SEO problem.

---

Most wellness content teams celebrate traffic.

They shouldn't.

A blog getting 5,000 monthly visits that never produces a demo request isn't a content win — it's a research library for people who'll never buy anything.

Here's what's actually happening: wellness content is almost always written for awareness. Generic topics. Broad audiences. Safe framing. It ranks because it targets high-volume keywords, not because it speaks to someone making a purchase decision.

The problem isn't the SEO. It's intent matching.

HR leaders evaluating a wellness platform don't need another "what is employee burnout" post. They need help making a case to the CFO, comparing platforms, or understanding what implementation actually looks like in a 500-person company.

Three shifts that move wellness content down-funnel:
– Replace "what is X" with "how to decide if X is right for your org"
– Add cost and ROI framing — HR leaders are accountable for budget
– Use case studies that name the decision, not just the outcome

Traffic is vanity. Conversion is the point.

What does your wellness content actually help HR leaders *decide*?
