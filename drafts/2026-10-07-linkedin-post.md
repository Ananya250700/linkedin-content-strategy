**Pillar:** Pillar 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** Most wellness content is designed to be read once. The highest-value pieces get forwarded — to a CFO, a CEO, a skeptical leadership team. Those are two different briefs, and most content teams aren't writing the second one.

---

Most wellness content is designed to be read. The best pieces in an HR content library get forwarded.

Those are two different briefs.

The HR leader who reads your burnout article and moves on is not the same as the one who sends it to their CFO before a program pitch. One piece lives in a browser tab. The other does political work inside an organization.

Here's a 3-question test I use before approving a wellness brief:

**1. Does it contain a number the HR leader couldn't find on their own?**
A benchmark. An ROI data point. A statistic specific enough to be useful in a presentation. Generic stats don't get forwarded.

**2. Does it give the reader language they can use in the next meeting they're already planning?**
Not the meeting they might have. The one already on the calendar — the leadership review, the budget conversation, the program proposal.

**3. Does it answer the question a skeptic in the room would raise?**
"Is this proven to work?" "What does this cost?" "Why now?" If your content doesn't address the pushback, it won't survive the conversation it was forwarded into.

Two out of three and you have a piece that travels. Zero out of three and you have a page view.
