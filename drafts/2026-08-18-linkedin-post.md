# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-08-18

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I retired a high-traffic post — and what that decision actually looked like

---

A blog post getting 8,000 monthly visitors isn't automatically worth keeping.

Last quarter, I retired one of Vantage Fit's best-performing pages by traffic — and replaced it with something that got 60% fewer visits but moved prospects further down the funnel.

Here's what drove that call.

Most B2B content teams optimize for the wrong signal first. Traffic tells you what people are finding. It doesn't tell you who they are or what they do next.

Before I touch any post for optimization, I run three checks:

**1. Search intent match** — Is the person landing here a buyer, or a student writing a research paper?
**2. BOFU gap** — Does this page answer a decision-stage question, or does it stop at awareness?
**3. Conversion path** — Can a reader take a meaningful next step, or does the journey dead-end here?

That high-traffic post failed all three. It ranked for a broad wellness keyword that HR interns use for assignments — not the term an HRBP searches when evaluating a wellness platform for 2,000 employees.

Retiring it wasn't a content loss. It was a resource reallocation.

Before your next content audit: are you optimizing for traffic volume or pipeline movement?
