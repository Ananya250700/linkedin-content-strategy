Most corporate wellness blogs have a traffic problem. Actually, they don't — that's the problem.

High traffic with zero demo requests is not a content win. It's a signal that you're publishing content employees want to read, not content HR buyers need to act on.

The gap usually looks like this:
— Your blog ranks for "how to reduce stress at work"
— 4,000 monthly visitors
— 0.3% lead rate

The problem isn't SEO. It's audience misalignment.

Employee-facing content — tips, habits, self-care lists — almost always outperforms decision-maker content in traffic. But HR leaders aren't Googling "how to build a wellness program." They're being handed a business problem and looking for proof that your solution moves the needle.

What actually converts:
→ Case studies anchored to measurable outcomes (engagement scores, absence rates)
→ Buyer-stage content that answers "how do we implement this," not "why wellness matters"
→ ROI frameworks a CHRO can walk into a CFO conversation with

If your top 10 posts are employee wellness tips, you're building the wrong audience.

What does your content-to-demo ratio look like? Curious if other B2B wellness marketers are tracking this gap.
