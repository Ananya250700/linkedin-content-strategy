# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-08-03

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Why polished wellness content ranks but doesn't convert — the permission gap between what employees need to hear and what brands are willing to say.

---

Most corporate wellness content is written to avoid discomfort.

No mention of burnout caused by unrealistic workloads. No honest look at why employees don't use your EAP. No acknowledgment that "wellness days" don't fix broken management.

The result? Content that ranks, gets shares, and changes nothing.

I've been building content for Vantage Fit for over a year, writing for HR leaders across industries. The pieces that actually drive demo requests share one trait: they name the problem employees won't say out loud.

Not "here are 5 tips to manage stress." But "here's why your stress management program isn't being used — and what the data actually shows."

The difference isn't tone. It's permission.

Honest wellness content gives HR leaders permission to name what's already true in their organization. Polished content pretends everything is fine. The honest version says: here's what's actually happening, and here's a path forward.

If your wellness content isn't converting, ask yourself: is it telling HR leaders something they already know — or something they've been afraid to say out loud?

That's the gap most wellness brands never close.
