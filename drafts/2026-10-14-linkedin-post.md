# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-10-14

**Pillar:** Pillar 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** Most wellness content briefs optimize for the keyword, not the decision HR leaders need to make after reading — a 3-part briefing shift fixes this.

---

Most wellness blog briefs start and end with a keyword.

Target query: "corporate wellness programs for remote teams." Search volume: 1,200/month. Difficulty: medium. That's the brief.

The problem: a brief built entirely around a keyword optimizes for the moment someone clicks — not the moment they need to act.

HR leaders reading that post don't just need information. They need something to do with it: a question to ask a potential vendor, a metric to pull before a CFO conversation, a framework to take into a leadership review. If the content doesn't give them that, it drives traffic and converts nothing.

Here's the briefing shift that changes this:

**1. Define the decision, not just the topic.**
What specific decision is the HR leader trying to make? Not "they want to learn about remote wellness" — what are they weighing, and against what?

**2. Map the internal objection.**
After they finish reading, what pushback will they face when they try to act? Build the content to preemptively answer that.

**3. Name the actionable exit.**
What should they be able to do after reading this? If your answer is "understand the topic better," that's awareness content. Give them a concrete next step.

A brief with those three answers produces content HR leaders save, forward to their leadership team, and return to during vendor evaluation.

Traffic is a metric. Usefulness is a strategy.
