# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-16

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** I killed a post ranking #3 for its keyword — because ranking ≠ reaching the right reader.

---

I deleted a post that ranked #3 for its primary keyword.

Not because it stopped performing. It was pulling traffic every week. The problem was what kind of traffic.

The post was a general explainer — "what is an employee wellness program." Informational, top-of-funnel, broad. It ranked well because the competition was thin and we'd built strong internal links to it.

But when I looked at downstream behavior: near-zero time on secondary pages. No clicks to our platform pages. No email signups. Nobody was moving.

The readers it attracted were employees trying to understand wellness at their own company. Not HR leaders evaluating vendors.

So I did two things.

First, I rewrote the bottom half to speak directly to HR buyers — added a benchmark table, a decision checklist, and a CTA to the implementation guide.

Second, I consolidated the original page's backlink equity into a new pillar built for procurement-stage intent.

Traffic dropped 30%. Qualified engagement went up.

That's the thing about content optimization: ranking is an output metric. Intent alignment is the input metric that actually drives pipeline.

If you're optimizing posts without auditing who's actually reading them — you're polishing a window someone else is looking through.
