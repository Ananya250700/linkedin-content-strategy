**Pillar:** Pillar 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing about (most wellness content teams don't have one)

---

Most wellness content teams don't have a filter. They have a content calendar and a list of "wellness trends."

That's why so much corporate wellness content reads like a press release — it's approved, not strategic.

Before I write any wellness topic at Vantage Fit, I run it through three questions:

**1. Does it address a real HR pain point — or just a surface-level topic?**
"Mental health at work" is a topic. "Why your EAP has 3% utilization and what to do about it" is a pain point. HR leaders don't need content that names a problem they already know. They need content that moves them toward a decision.

**2. Can this content do something a Google search can't?**
Generic wellness content competes with 10,000 identical articles. The only content worth writing has a specific POV, a real example, or a framework that didn't exist before.

**3. Is there a clear next step for the reader?**
If you can't answer "what does the reader do after reading this?" — the content has no function. Awareness content has a role, but only if awareness leads somewhere.

Most wellness brands skip this filter entirely. The result: a content library full of traffic with no pipeline.
