# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-27

**Pillar:** Pillar 3: Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing about — challenging how most content topics get chosen (trend-chasing, volume-hunting) vs. mapping to actual HR decisions

---

Most wellness content topics get chosen because someone saw a competitor publish something. Or because a keyword tool said it had volume.

Neither of those is a reason.

Here's the 3-part filter I use before writing a single word about any wellness topic at Vantage Fit:

**1. Does it map to a decision an HR leader actually has to make?**
Not "is burnout a real problem" — everyone knows it is. But "how do I build a case for a mental health program to my CFO?" That's a decision. Write for the decision.

**2. Does it exist in a cluster that builds authority, or does it stand alone?**
Standalone posts rank slower, attract less qualified traffic, and convert rarely. If a topic doesn't connect forward and backward to content you already own, it's not ready to publish — it's ready to plan around.

**3. Can you say something your competitors haven't?**
If your draft reads like the top 3 Google results, it adds nothing. Every wellness topic worth covering has a non-obvious angle. Find it before you brief the piece.

Most of the wellness content I see fails filter #1. It's educational, not decision-enabling.

What filter do you apply before greenlighting a content topic?
