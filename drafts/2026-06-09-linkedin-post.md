# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-09

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I killed a high-traffic post — and the 3-step process I used to replace it with one that actually converts

---

A blog ranking in position 3 doesn't deserve to stay just because it ranks.

Last year I retired a post getting 3,000+ visits a month.

It ranked for "employee wellness tips." Had backlinks. Kept compounding traffic.

I killed it anyway.

The traffic was high-intent but wrong-intent. People searching that phrase are mostly employees looking for personal advice — not HR leaders evaluating a wellness platform. When I mapped it against our actual buyer journey, it touched zero conversion moments. No demo requests. No pillar referrals. No qualified signups. Just volume with nowhere useful to go.

What I did instead:

1. Stripped it to a redirect stub
2. Merged the salvageable content into a cluster post targeting "employee wellness programs" — the phrase HR decision-makers actually search
3. Rebuilt internal links to funnel toward BOFU pages

Three months later: the new post ranked higher and drove qualified traffic.

High traffic is not the goal. Qualified traffic is.

If you're managing B2B wellness content: when did you last check whether your top posts are reaching the right people — or just reaching more people?
