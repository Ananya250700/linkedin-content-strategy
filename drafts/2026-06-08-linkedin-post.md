# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-08

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness blog ranking #1 isn't a win — it's a buyer intent problem in disguise.

---

Your wellness blog ranking #1 for "employee burnout tips" is not a content win.

It's a traffic problem in disguise.

Most wellness content is optimized for search volume, not buyer intent. That post gets 5,000 visits a month — from employees managing their own stress, not HR leaders evaluating a wellness platform.

The person Googling "how to reduce burnout" is not the person who signs a demo request.

So the traffic looks healthy. The pipeline stays empty.

The fix isn't more content. It's one hard question before you start writing:

"Who is actually searching this — and do they have budget authority?"

If the answer is "an individual contributor," that post has limited commercial value, no matter how well it ranks. It might build awareness. It won't generate qualified leads.

The content that actually moves HR decision-makers is specific: implementation guides, benchmarking data, case studies from their industry. Those don't rank for 50,000 monthly searches. But they convert.

Most wellness content teams are building for vanity metrics without realizing it.

What metric is your content strategy actually optimized for — traffic or pipeline?
