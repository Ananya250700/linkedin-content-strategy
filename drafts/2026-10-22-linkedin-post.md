**Pillar:** 4 — Vantage Fit Work Amplification
**Angle:** The design assumption flaw in corporate wellness: most programs are built for desk workers, not the 80% of the workforce that isn't.

---

Most corporate wellness programs are designed for someone who has a desk.

That's roughly 20% of the global workforce.

The other 80% — manufacturing workers, healthcare staff, logistics teams — are expected to benefit from programs built for people who can step away for a meditation break or log into a wellness app between meetings.

I spent weeks researching this for Vantage Fit's manufacturing wellness implementation guide. The gap isn't just access — it's design assumption. Wellness vendors assume digital touchpoints, flexible schedules, and self-directed behavior change. Frontline workers get none of that.

What actually works in manufacturing environments:
→ Shift-level micro-check-ins, not app-based logging
→ Manager-led wellness conversations, not anonymous surveys
→ Physical health metrics tied to shift safety, not generic step counts

The companies getting this right aren't using fancier platforms. They're rethinking who the program is actually for.

If you're in HR at an industrial or logistics company — is your current wellness program built for your workers, or built for your head office?
