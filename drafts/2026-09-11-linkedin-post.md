# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-11

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** The most-trafficked wellness blogs often drive the least pipeline — because high-volume wellness keywords are searched by employees seeking personal relief, not by CHROs evaluating platforms. Traffic is not demand.

---

The most-trafficked wellness blogs often drive the least pipeline. That's not a coincidence — it's a structural problem.

High-volume wellness keywords — burnout, stress management, work-life balance — are primarily searched by employees looking for personal relief, not by HR leaders evaluating platforms.

B2B wellness platforms have gotten very good at ranking for those terms. And then they're confused when traffic doesn't convert.

The problem: content strategy was built around keyword volume, not buyer intent. The employee Googling "how to avoid burnout" is not the same person who submits a demo request. You've attracted the right topic audience — not the right buyer.

When I map content clusters for Vantage Fit, I separate traffic by query type: informational (employee-facing), navigational (vendor-aware), decisional (evaluation-stage). Most wellness platform content libraries are heavy on the first, almost absent on the third.

You can have 50,000 monthly sessions on your wellness blog and zero of them from a CHRO actively building a business case.

Traffic is not demand. Ranking is not pipeline.

If you pulled your wellness blog's session data and filtered by company size and role — what percentage of your audience is actually your buyer?
