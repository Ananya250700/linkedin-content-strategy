**Pillar:** Pillar 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** The content brief is where B2B wellness content fails before anyone writes a word — most briefs answer "what to write," not who's reading or what they need to believe to act.

---

Most B2B wellness content briefs are topic briefs. They tell a writer what to cover, not who to write for or what that person needs to believe by the end.

The result: technically accurate content that nobody with buying power reads past the introduction.

When I write a brief for Vantage Fit content, I add four fields that most briefs don't include:

**Reader role.** Not just "HR professional" — which HR professional? A CHRO building a business case, an HR manager researching vendors, or an employee wellness lead trying to justify a budget? Each needs a different article.

**Decision context.** What is this reader deciding, or about to decide? Wellness content that doesn't connect to a real decision is entertainment, not strategy.

**Belief gap.** What does the reader currently believe that might be blocking them from taking the next step? The brief should name it. The content should close it.

**Next logical step.** Not a generic CTA — a specific, frictionless action that makes sense at exactly this stage.

A brief that answers these four questions produces content that gets bookmarked and forwarded. A topic brief produces a blog that ranks for nobody who matters.
