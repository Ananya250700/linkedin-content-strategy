# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-15

**Pillar:** Pillar 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** The decision to retire a high-traffic post — what signals tell you traffic is a liability, not an asset, and the three-signal framework for making that call without gut feel.

---

A post with 4,000 monthly visitors and zero pipeline influence in 12 months isn't performing. It's occupying space.

Most B2B content teams hold onto high-traffic posts because traffic feels like proof of something. It is — but not always of what you need. Traffic from searchers who aren't your ICP, on topics that don't connect to any decision your buyer is actually making, inflates your cluster metrics without moving your business.

At Vantage Fit, I use three signals before deciding to optimize, repurpose, or retire a post:

**Intent match.** Is the searcher our ICP — an HR leader evaluating wellness platforms — or an employee looking for stress tips? Both might read the same article. Only one might become a lead.

**Conversion path.** After reading, is there a logical next step that connects to a decision? If not, the post ends at the read.

**Cluster contribution.** Does this post support a decision-stage piece, or is it an island with no connections to where our buyers actually evaluate?

High-traffic posts that fail all three don't get kept out of sentiment. They get repurposed toward a cluster that earns its place — or retired.

Traffic is not a strategy. What it's bringing in is.
