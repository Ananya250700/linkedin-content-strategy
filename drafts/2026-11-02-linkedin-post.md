# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-02

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content that drives traffic but not conversions — because it's written to reassure, not to help HR leaders decide.

---

Your wellness content is getting traffic. It's not generating anything else.

This isn't a distribution problem. It's a positioning problem.

Most wellness vendors write content designed to reassure HR leaders — "here's why wellness matters," "10 tips for employee wellbeing." It ranks. People find it. They leave.

The content that actually moves HR leaders toward a decision does something different: it names a specific problem they're sitting with. Not "burnout is real" — but "here's why your current EAP has a 3% utilization rate, and what that tells you about your program design."

The difference is specificity. Generic wellness content speaks to everyone. Decision-enabling content speaks to a specific HR leader with a specific problem at a specific stage.

At Vantage Fit, the shift that moved our content from traffic to pipeline was writing toward the decision, not the awareness. Less "why wellness matters," more "how do you know if your wellness program is working."

If your wellness blog is ranking and not converting, ask yourself: am I writing to inform, or am I writing to help someone make a call?

That's the whole gap.
