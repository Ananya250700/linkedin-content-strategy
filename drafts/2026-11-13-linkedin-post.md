# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-11-13

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** What I actually look at before optimizing a blog — it's not keywords first

---

Most content audits start with traffic data.
That's the wrong starting point.

Before I touch a keyword or update a paragraph, I ask three questions:

**1. What decision is this post supposed to help the reader make?**
Not "what's it about" — what decision. A blog titled "Best Employee Wellness Programs" is useless unless it's structured to help an HR leader evaluate vendors, build a case for budget, or scope a rollout. If it does none of those things, the traffic numbers are vanity.

**2. Where does it sit in the buyer journey — and is the funnel path working?**
High-traffic TOFU posts that never link anything deeper aren't assets. They're leaks. If optimization means fixing the conversion path, that matters more than adding three new subheadings.

**3. Would I retire this post if I couldn't see its traffic?**
Surprisingly honest filter. If the answer is yes, optimization is a Band-Aid.

Most wellness content teams optimize for rankings. The better question is: does this post move someone toward a decision?

That's what separates content that performs from content that converts.

What do you check before you start optimizing?
