**Angle:** What building the manufacturing wellness content cluster revealed about how companies measure wellness ROI — and why most are measuring the wrong thing.

---

Most employee wellness programs track participation. Almost none track behavior change.

I noticed this gap while building the content cluster around manufacturing workforce wellness for Vantage Fit. Company after company measures enrollment in wellness challenges. Almost none measure whether employees actually changed how they manage stress, fatigue, or workload after the program ends.

The problem isn't data. It's positioning.

When a wellness platform sells "engagement," HR buys participation metrics. When it sells "behavior change," HR has to ask harder questions — and expect better answers. Most vendors default to the easy sell because it's easier to report.

The piece I built for Vantage Fit's manufacturing vertical leans into this gap. It doesn't just cover which programs to run on the floor. It covers what to measure if you want to make a case to a CFO, not just a people ops team.

If you're designing wellness for a non-desk workforce, it's worth your time.
