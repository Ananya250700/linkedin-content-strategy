# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-29

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content drives traffic but not pipeline — because it's written to look safe internally, not to help HR leaders make decisions.

---

Your wellness blog gets 3,000 visits a month. Zero demo requests.

That's not an SEO problem. It's a content problem.

Most wellness vendors write content that ranks. Broad keywords, clean headers, optimized structure. And it performs — on paper. But the HR leader reading it isn't finding what they came for.

Because it's written to look safe internally, not to help an HR director make a decision.

"Building a culture of wellness" tells them nothing. No benchmarks. No honest acknowledgment that the current program might not be working. No path from problem to implementation.

The content that converts does something different: it names what the HR leader is already thinking but no one in wellness marketing will say out loud.

Burnout data they can take to a CFO. Honest assessments of what generic wellness programs fail to deliver. Frameworks that assume a skeptical audience, not a pre-sold one.

Traffic is a vanity metric if the content doesn't help someone justify a budget or change a program.

Most wellness content isn't underperforming because of weak SEO. It's underperforming because it was written for the algorithm and not the decision-maker.

What's one wellness topic your team avoids because it feels too uncomfortable to publish?
