# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-15

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Mental Health Awareness Month content performs well — and quietly trains your algorithm to reach the wrong audience right before procurement season.

---

Most wellness brands put out their best content in May.

They also run the thinnest Q2 pipelines.

Mental Health Awareness Month is the highest-traffic period for wellness vendors. Every brand publishes. Impressions spike. Engagement looks strong.

And then HR leaders go quiet.

Here's what's actually happening. Awareness-month content is written for employees — people searching for personal coping resources, not CHROs evaluating platforms. The content performs with exactly the wrong audience.

LinkedIn's algorithm rewards that engagement. It optimizes your distribution for more of the same. By June, your content is surfacing consistently to individual contributors, not People leaders.

You've trained the algorithm on the wrong reader at the worst possible time.

Because the real procurement cycle for a wellness platform runs Q4 and Q1 — when HR budgets are set, RFPs go out, and shortlists get built. The brands that win those conversations aren't the ones who went viral for World Mental Health Day. They're the ones publishing benchmarking reports, implementation guides, and ROI frameworks in Q3 — when HR leaders are doing pre-budget research.

Calendar relevance isn't the same as buyer relevance.

What does your wellness content calendar look like in August?
