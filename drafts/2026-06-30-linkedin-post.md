# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-30

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I killed a high-traffic post — and what the data showed about what wellness content actually converts

---

High traffic doesn't mean the post is working.

Last quarter, I retired a blog pulling 2,000+ monthly visits. It ranked on page 1. It was well-written. And it was producing zero demo requests.

The post targeted a top-of-funnel keyword — "benefits of employee wellness programs." Broad, educational, and completely misaligned with what our buyers actually needed before making a decision.

Here's the thing about wellness content: the topics that get searched most are usually least likely to convert. HR directors Googling "benefits of wellness programs" are researchers, not buyers. They've already sold themselves on wellness. They're trying to convince their C-suite.

What they actually needed was content that helps them build the business case — ROI data, vendor comparisons, implementation cost frameworks.

So I killed the post, redirected the URL to a decision-stage piece, and rebuilt the cluster around the real question: *"How do I get leadership to approve a wellness budget?"*

Traffic dropped 40%. Pipeline conversations went up.

If you're measuring your wellness content strategy by traffic, you're optimizing for the wrong signal.

What metric is your team actually using to evaluate content performance?
