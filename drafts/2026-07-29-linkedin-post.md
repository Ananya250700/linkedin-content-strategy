# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-29

**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-question brief framework that separates wellness content that informs from content that drives decisions

---

Most HR content briefs describe a topic. The best ones describe a decision.

There's a difference between "write about burnout prevention" and "help a mid-sized HR manager make the case to their CFO for a wellness budget increase." One produces information. The other produces leverage.

After briefing dozens of wellness pieces at Vantage Fit, I've landed on three questions that separate content that gets saved from content that gets actioned:

**1. What decision does this content need to support?**
Not "what should the reader learn" — what are they choosing? Evaluating vendors, justifying spend, building a program. Name the decision.

**2. What is the real objection blocking that decision?**
Budget? Proof of ROI? Leadership buy-in? The brief should address the objection directly — not dance around it with generic wellness benefits.

**3. What does "convinced" look like?**
If the content works, what does the reader do next? Schedule a call? Forward the piece to their CHRO? Write that into the brief. If you can't describe the next action, the content has no job.

Most wellness content fails the brief test at question one. It describes a subject — not a decision in progress.

What does your content brief process look like — do you start with the topic or the reader's situation?
