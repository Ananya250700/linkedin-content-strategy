# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-22

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness blog drives traffic but not demo requests — because you're writing for Google, not for HR decision-makers.

---

Your wellness blog has 10,000 monthly visitors. Your demo pipeline is empty.

That gap isn't a distribution problem. It's a content problem.

Most B2B wellness platforms write blogs that Google loves but HR leaders skim past. High volume. Generic topics. Safe language. Content that carefully avoids naming burnout as a leadership failure — or explaining why wellness programs get ignored in the first place.

Because naming the real problem feels risky. So instead, brands publish "5 Ways to Support Employee Wellbeing" for the hundredth time.

Here's what actually works: content that speaks to the problem HR leaders won't say out loud in a board meeting.

EAP utilization sits at 3–6% industry-wide. Most wellness programs are announced after a difficult news cycle and never measured afterward. HR leaders know this. They're not looking for validation — they're looking for someone who gets it and has a path forward.

When your content names the uncomfortable reality instead of soft-pedaling it, HR leaders stop skimming and start reading. That's the difference between content that ranks and content that converts.

Traffic is a vanity metric when the reader doesn't recognize their problem in your words.

What's one wellness topic you've avoided publishing because it felt too risky?
