**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing about

---

Most wellness content topics get picked because they're trending — not because they move HR leaders toward a decision.

Here's a 3-part filter I use before writing anything for Vantage Fit:

**1. Is there a real decision behind this topic?**
If the reader can finish the post and still not know what to do differently, it's an awareness post dressed up as advice. Good wellness content changes what HR leaders ask for in their next vendor conversation.

**2. Does this connect a visible symptom to a structural cause?**
HR leaders already know employees are burned out. What they need is the link between burnout and, say, how their recognition programs are — or aren't — designed. Surface-level is safe. Structural is useful.

**3. Would this embarrass a vendor if they published it?**
The best wellness content is honest enough to feel uncomfortable. If a wellness platform could brand it and post it without changing a word, it's probably too polished to be trusted.

Topics that pass all three: worth your content calendar. Topics that pass only one: worth a social post at most.

The mistake most wellness content teams make isn't choosing wrong topics — it's choosing topics without a filter at all.
