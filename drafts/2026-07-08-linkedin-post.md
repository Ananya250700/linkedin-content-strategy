**Pillar:** 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** Most wellness content briefs are built for the algorithm, not the decision-maker — here's what a brief looks like when it's actually built for the reader.

---

Most wellness content briefs are built for Google, not for the person who has to make a call.

A keyword. A target length. A competitor to beat. That's the standard B2B brief.

What it skips: the HR leader reading this piece is usually mid-decision. They're justifying a budget, building a business case, or trying to figure out if this problem is even worth solving. The brief should be built around what they need to do next — not what the algorithm needs.

When I build a brief for Vantage Fit wellness content, I add four fields that most templates don't have:

**Decision point:** What is this reader actually trying to decide? Not a persona — a specific call. "HR manager evaluating whether to launch a step challenge this quarter" beats "HR manager interested in wellness."

**The stall objection:** What's the real reason they haven't acted yet? Budget pushback, leadership skepticism, no benchmark data? The brief names it. The piece answers it.

**Proof type:** Data, case study, framework, or practitioner experience — each earns a different kind of trust. Picking the wrong one undercuts a well-researched piece.

**Next action:** Not a CTA. The literal thing the reader should do when they close the tab.

SEO gets a reader to the page. The brief determines whether they leave with something useful or just informed.
