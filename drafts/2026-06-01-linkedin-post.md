# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-06-01

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** What HR leaders actually want to read vs. what wellness vendors publish — and why the gap is killing conversions

---

Most wellness content is written for the brand's comfort, not the HR leader's reality.

Here's what I see constantly as a B2B wellness content strategist: companies publishing spotless blogs about "building a culture of care" while their HR teams are managing active burnout crises, tracking absenteeism spikes, and fielding exit interview feedback they can't share publicly.

The content avoids all of that.

It's aspirational. It's branded. It's safe.

And HR leaders scroll past it.

What actually gets read — saved, forwarded in Slack, referenced in leadership meetings — is content that names the problem before offering the solution. Content that acknowledges "your employees may not trust your wellness program because they've lived through two rounds of layoffs" instead of leading with ROI statistics.

The formula is simple, but most brands won't go there:

Name the uncomfortable truth → Acknowledge the real constraint HR is working within → Then give a practical path forward.

That shift is what turns a wellness blog from brand content into something an HR leader actually bookmarks.

If you're publishing wellness content right now: are you writing for the HR professional who's struggling, or for the version of HR you wish existed?
