# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-10-16

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Wellness content that ranks well still fails to convert — because most of it is written to educate, not to help HR leaders decide.

---

Your wellness blog is getting traffic. And your pipeline is still quiet.

That's not a distribution problem. That's a content problem.

Most corporate wellness content is built for search volume, not decision-making. It answers "what is employee burnout" when your buyer is already past that. They're not looking to learn. They're looking to justify a purchase to their CFO.

The content that converts looks different:
- It names the specific problem the HR leader is sitting with right now
- It shows what happens if they don't act — in real business terms, not wellness jargon
- It gives them a framework they can take into a leadership meeting

The mistake I see constantly: wellness brands treat the blog as an awareness tool and then wonder why demo requests don't follow. Awareness content and decision-enabling content are not the same thing — and most wellness content teams only build one of them.

If your top-ranking post isn't moving anyone closer to a conversation, the ranking is vanity.

What's the last piece of content you read that actually changed how you thought about a vendor's product?
