# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-08-24

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness blog ranks on page one but never generates a demo request — the gap between SEO performance and conversion is a positioning problem, not a keyword problem.

---

Your wellness blog ranks on page one. HR teams read it, bookmark it, share it in Slack.

And then nothing. No demo requests. No sales conversations.

This happens more than wellness brands admit.

The problem isn't the SEO. It's the positioning.

Most wellness content is written to comfort, not to challenge. It validates what HR leaders already believe — "employee wellbeing matters" — instead of showing them what they're missing or getting wrong.

Content that converts does something uncomfortable: it names a failure the reader already recognizes. It says "here's the gap in your current wellness program" before it says "here's what to do instead."

That shift — from reassurance to diagnosis — is the difference between content that earns a save and content that starts a sales conversation.

A simple test: read your last three wellness articles. Do they challenge the reader's current approach? Or do they confirm that whatever they're already doing is basically fine?

If it's mostly the latter, you're producing educational content — not conversion content.

What's one piece of wellness content that actually moved someone from reader to conversation?
