# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-27

**Pillar:** Pillar 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Your wellness blog drives traffic but nobody's booking a demo — because most wellness content is built for reach, not decisions.

---

Your wellness blog has 10,000 monthly visitors. Nobody's booking a demo.

That's not a traffic problem. It's a trust gap — and most wellness content teams don't see it because they're optimizing for the wrong metric.

Here's what's happening: wellness content performs well at the top of the funnel. "Signs of employee burnout." "How to improve workplace wellbeing." Generic, helpful, high-volume. Google loves it. HR managers save it. Nobody buys from it.

The problem is the bottom of the funnel. BOFU wellness content — the kind that helps a CHRO justify budget, compare vendors, or write a business case — barely exists. Because it's uncomfortable to write. It requires naming real tradeoffs, acknowledging limitations, and trusting the reader to make a decision.

Most wellness vendors write for reach. The brands that convert write for decisions.

Three questions I ask before publishing any wellness piece:
- Does this help an HR leader decide — or just feel informed?
- Does it address a real objection, or sidestep it?
- Would a CHRO share this with their CFO?

If the answer is no to all three, it's traffic content. Not conversion content.

Are you building one or the other — and do you actually know which?
