# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-09-28

**Pillar:** 1 — Honest Takes on Wellness Content
**Angle:** Most wellness content is built for the searcher, not the buyer — and that's why it doesn't convert.

---

Most corporate wellness blogs rank on Google but lose HR leaders by page two.

Not because the writing is bad. Because the intent is wrong.

When I audit wellness content for conversion, I look at one thing first: is this post written for a person searching, or for a person deciding?

There's a difference.

A searcher wants to understand a concept — burnout, EAP utilization, employee engagement scores. A decision-maker wants to know if your solution is worth their budget and political capital.

Most wellness blogs are written entirely for the searcher. They explain, define, and inform. Then they drop a CTA that says "book a demo" — and wonder why the conversion rate is flat.

The content that actually moves HR buyers does something different: it names a specific problem, in the language the HR leader uses internally, with enough specificity that they feel understood — not just informed.

Traffic is not traction. Understanding the intent gap between informing and convincing is the first step to building wellness content that does both.

How much of your wellness content is written for the searcher — and how much for the buyer?
