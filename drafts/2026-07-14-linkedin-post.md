# LinkedIn Post Draft — 2026-07-14

**Pillar:** 2 — Behind-the-Process: B2B Content Strategy
**Angle:** Why I retired a 2,000-visitor post — and the 3-signal framework that made the decision obvious

---

Most content teams treat high-traffic posts as untouchable.

Earlier this year, I flagged a Vantage Fit blog pulling over 2,000 monthly visitors for retirement. The traffic wasn't the problem — the signal behind it was.

Three checks I run before I keep, update, or consolidate any B2B content piece:

**1. Intent alignment**
Who is actually landing here? Not who the keyword implies — who the behavior data reveals. Informational queries from job seekers and students are not the same as decision-stage queries from HR managers running a wellness budget conversation. Same keyword, different audience, wrong page.

**2. Conversion path**
What does someone do after reading this? If the answer is "close the tab," the post is filling a content calendar slot, not supporting a decision. Useful B2B content has a logical next step that serves the reader — not just a CTA that serves the brand.

**3. Cannibalization**
Is this post splitting ranking authority with a stronger page targeting the same intent? Consolidation almost always outperforms updates when two pieces are competing for the same query.

The 2,000-visitor post was retired. The consolidated piece now ranks higher, attracts the right audience, and has a cleaner path forward.

Traffic is a starting point, not a verdict.

What metric do you actually trust when you're deciding what to do with an underperforming post?
