**Pillar:** Pillar 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is actually worth writing

---

Most wellness content calendars are full. Most wellness content is useless.

The problem isn't volume. It's selection. HR teams and wellness vendors pick topics based on what sounds good — not what actually moves someone to act.

Here's the 3-part filter I use before writing any wellness piece:

**1. Does it name a real tension?**
Not "why wellness matters" — that's already assumed. Does this topic surface a specific friction an HR leader actually feels? Burnout measurement, ROI justification, employee skepticism toward programs. The uncomfortable topics are the ones worth writing.

**2. Does it help someone make a decision?**
Wellness content fails when it's awareness-only. If a reader finishes the piece and can't do anything differently tomorrow, the content isn't doing its job. Every topic should connect to a specific decision: what to measure, how to pitch a program, when to retire a benefit.

**3. Is there a non-obvious angle?**
If the first search result already covers it, you're writing for traffic, not authority. Good wellness content should say something the reader hasn't seen in the last 12 articles they skimmed.

Run any proposed topic through these three. If it fails more than one, it's not worth your content team's time.

What's the wellness topic your team keeps avoiding? That's usually the one worth writing.
