Most workplace wellness content is written for knowledge workers.

Desk job. Flexible hours. Slack access. Mental health app on their phone.

That describes less than a third of the global workforce.

I built Vantage Fit's implementation guide for manufacturing wellness specifically because almost no wellness content addresses physical labor environments — shift schedules, floor safety compliance, no personal devices during work hours, supervisors who aren't trained to recognize burnout.

The brief: write something an HR manager at a mid-size plant could use on Monday morning. Not inspire. Enable.

Three decisions shaped it differently from standard wellness content:

**Lead with constraints, not benefits.** Manufacturing HR doesn't need convincing that wellness matters — they need a program that works inside a 12-hour rotating shift.

**Build the supervisor layer.** HR can't be on the floor. If supervisors don't know how to observe and escalate, the program never reaches the employees who need it most.

**Treat participation as a design problem.** If your wellness initiative requires voluntary signups on a personal phone, you've already excluded your highest-risk employees.

It became one of Vantage Fit's top-performing pieces in manufacturing category queries.

The lesson: specificity earns trust that broad content never will. HR leaders see through generic wellness advice faster than almost any other buyer.
