**Pillar:** Pillar 3 — Frameworks for HR Content Leaders
**Angle:** A 3-part filter for deciding if a wellness topic is worth writing — most teams skip this and end up with a large library that serves the wrong audience at the wrong funnel stage.

---

Most wellness content topics get chosen because someone on the team said "this feels timely." That's not a strategy. That's a content backlog with no purpose.

Before I write a single word for a wellness blog, I run it through three questions:

**1. Who is actually reading this?**
Employees searching for stress relief — or HR leaders evaluating a platform? Most wellness blogs are written for employees but positioned as B2B content. The audience mismatch shows in the pipeline data.

**2. Does this topic live where the decision happens?**
Informational content educates. Decision-stage content helps someone build a business case. If you can't name the specific decision this post supports, it's unlikely to drive a demo request.

**3. Does your platform have something unique to say here?**
If a generic HR blogger could write the same piece, your brand adds no differentiation. The best wellness content should require your proprietary data, your case studies, your implementation experience — not just keyword research.

Most teams skip this filter and publish based on what's trending. The result: a large content library that ranks, but doesn't convert.

Run your next 10 planned topics through these questions. You'll likely cut half. What survives will work harder.
